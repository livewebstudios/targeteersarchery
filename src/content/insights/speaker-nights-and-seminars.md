Every so often we clear the front of the store, put out chairs, and somebody who has spent their
life doing this stands where the counter usually is.

There are photographs of these nights all over the office. They are some of the best evenings this
building has.

## What has happened here

Brian Quaca, better known as Pig Man, has been here. Hoyt and Easton have run technical seminars on this range.
Over the years a long list of people from the archery and hunting world have come through, and a
lot of them ended up in a photograph on our wall.

The room is usually full. Sometimes uncomfortably full, which is a nice problem.

## Why we keep doing it

Because there is a category of knowledge that does not survive being written down.

You can read about arrow spine and shot placement and cam timing. What you cannot get from
reading is somebody who has done a thing ten thousand times telling you which part of it actually
matters, and answering the follow-up question you did not know you had.

A seminar is an hour where the follow-up question is allowed. That is the whole value.

## What a night is like

Informal. Nobody is selling from the front of the room, and if it starts feeling like a sales
pitch we have not booked it well.

Usually there is a talk, then a long question section that runs longer than the talk, then people
standing around the bow wall for another forty minutes not going home. That last part is arguably
the event.

If it is a technical seminar, bring your bow. Several of the most useful moments we have had
involved somebody's actual bow on the bench in front of thirty people.

## How to find out about the next one

They are announced on the [calendar](/calendar/), on Facebook, and in the newsletter, and the
newsletter usually goes out first, because Lisa writes it and Lisa knows before the rest of us do.

Around seven thousand people are on that list. Signing up is the single best way to not miss a
night that fills.

## If you want to bring a group

We also run private evenings for groups: scout troops working on a badge, team outings, corporate
nights, clubs. Equipment, range time, instruction and targets, with somebody explaining what is
happening rather than just handing out bows.
