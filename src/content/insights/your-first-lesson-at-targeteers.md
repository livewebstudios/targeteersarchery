The most common thing people say when they call about a lesson is some version of "I have never
done this and I do not own anything."

Good. That is the entire point of the lesson rate that includes our equipment. You do not need to
buy a thing to find out whether you like archery.

Here is what the hour actually looks like.

## Before you come in

Book ahead. All lessons are booked in advance and there is a deposit, because the lanes get
committed to leagues, camp and events and we cannot hold one on a maybe.

Wear closed-toe shoes. That is not a style rule, it is a range rule, and we will turn away
sandals. Wear something that lets you raise both arms without a sleeve bunching at the elbow.
Leave the long earrings and the loose bracelet at home; a bowstring finds them.

If you are seven or older, you are old enough. Under seven, we would rather you wait a year.

## The first ten minutes

You will not shoot immediately, and that is deliberate.

Your instructor measures you. Draw length, which is mostly a function of your wingspan, and a
draw weight you can hold comfortably rather than the most you can haul back once. Beginners
consistently want more weight than they should have, and it is the fastest way to build a bad
habit that takes a year to unlearn.

Then you get fitted with a bow, an arm guard, and a tab or a release. Then the safety brief:
where you stand, when you shoot, when you walk down to the target, and what the range commands
mean. It is short and it is not optional.

## The next twenty minutes

You shoot at close range. Very close... closer than feels dignified. There is a reason for that.

At three or five yards, everybody hits the target. That takes the outcome off the table and lets
you and your instructor pay attention to the only thing that matters at the start: the shot
itself. Stance, hook, anchor, the back tension that makes a release clean instead of a flinch.

If you spend your first session watching arrows miss at twenty yards you will learn to aim
harder, which is the opposite of what you want.

## The rest of the hour

Distance goes back a step at a time, once the shot holds together. Some people are at ten yards
by the end of the first hour and some are still at five, and neither of those is a verdict on
anything.

Somewhere in there you will put an arrow exactly where you meant to, and you will feel the
difference between that arrow and the others. That feeling is what everybody in this building is
still chasing.

## Half hour or full hour?

Half an hour is enough to find out if you like it. An hour is better if you want to leave having
actually built something. Both include range time, the equipment and the instructor.

## Afterwards

Nobody is going to walk you to the bow wall and start selling. If you want to look, the showroom
is right there and we will happily talk. If you want to go home and think about it, that is a
completely normal outcome and we will see you when we see you.

If you did like it, the usual next steps are a few more lessons, then a
[league](/leagues/) once you can hold a shot together. Leagues are where this gets social, and
the social part is why people are still shooting here thirty years later.
