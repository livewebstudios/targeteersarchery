We make custom strings here. It is the thing this shop has always specialized in, and it is the
service most customers understand the least.

So: what is actually different about a set built for your bow, and what do you feel?

## What a string has to survive

Every shot puts the string through a violent, repeated cycle. It stretches, it snaps back, it
takes friction at the cams, at the rest, and wherever it contacts a cable guard.

Two things follow. Strings stretch. That is not a defect, it is the material behaving normally.
And strings wear at specific points rather than evenly.

## What stretch does to your bow

As a string stretches, everything downstream moves.

Draw length creeps longer. Peep rotation shifts so you are no longer looking through the hole at
anchor. Cam timing drifts, which means the two cams stop rolling over together, which shows up as
inconsistent arrow flight that you cannot fix with sight adjustment.

None of it happens overnight. It happens over months, and you adapt to it without noticing. That
is why people describe a bow going out of tune as "it just feels different lately."

## What a good build does about it

**Pre-stretch.** A string that has been built under tension and held there has already done most
of the stretching it is going to do. It settles in a few dozen shots instead of a few hundred, and
then it stays where you put it.

**Length and layup to your bow.** Not a generic length off a peg. Built to spec, with the right
strand count for the draw weight you actually shoot.

**Serving in the right places, at the right tension.** Center serving that fits your nocks: not
too tight, not loose enough to let the nock rattle. End servings that take the wear the cams put
on them. Serving separation is the single most common thing we see on strings that were sold as
finished.

**Peep and D-loop tied in and shot in.** The peep gets set to your anchor, then shot until it
holds rotation, then re-checked. That step alone is worth the money.

## What you feel

Honestly? On the first shot, not much. Nobody walks off the range saying a string changed their
life.

What you notice is over the next two months: the sight does not need chasing. The peep is where
it was last week. The bow that felt like it was drifting stops drifting.

Consistency is not dramatic. It is just the absence of the small annoyances you had learned to
tolerate.

## When to replace

Once a year if you shoot leagues or hunt every season. Sooner if you can see fuzzing, a separated
serving, discoloration, or if your peep has started rotating again after being set.

If you are having a full tune done anyway, do the strings first. Tuning a bow and then putting new
strings on it means tuning it twice.
